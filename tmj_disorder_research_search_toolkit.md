# TMJ Disorder Research Search Toolkit

**Scope:** Temporomandibular joint disorders, temporomandibular disorders, orofacial pain, masticatory muscle pain, bruxism, headache comorbidity, pain neuroscience, psychological factors, and behavioral/psychological treatment.

**Recommended abbreviation use:**  
- **TMD** = temporomandibular disorders  
- **TMJ** = temporomandibular joint  
- **OFP** = orofacial pain  
- **DC/TMD** = Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders  
- **RDC/TMD** = Research Diagnostic Criteria for Temporomandibular Disorders


- **Related articles**
- Sort by date
- Add keyword after title, e.g. `catastrophizing`, `headache`, `CBT`

### 17.4 Warning signs for weak evidence

Be cautious when a paper has:
- Very small sample size without power calculation
- Vague TMD diagnosis without DC/TMD or RDC/TMD
- Mixed TMD phenotypes without subgroup analysis
- Self-reported “TMJ pain” only, without clinical examination
- No control group for treatment claims
- Short follow-up only
- Inadequate blinding for procedural interventions
- Overreliance on occlusion-only explanations
- Strong claims about causality from cross-sectional data
- Unclear bruxism measurement method
- No adjustment for comorbid headache, neck pain, anxiety, depression, or sleep problems

## 22. Notes on Terminology

- Prefer **temporomandibular disorders** over **TMJ disorder** in academic searches because TMD covers joint, muscle, and related masticatory-system disorders.
- Use **TMJ disorder** as an additional free-text term because patients, clinicians, and older papers often use it.
- Use **orofacial pain** to capture broader pain literature that may include TMD.
- Use **masticatory muscle pain**, **myofascial TMD**, and **myogenous TMD** when focusing on muscle-dominant pain.
- Use **arthralgia**, **disc displacement**, **internal derangement**, and **osteoarthritis** when focusing on joint-dominant TMD.
- Use **DC/TMD** and **RDC/TMD** when searching for higher-quality diagnostic studies.
